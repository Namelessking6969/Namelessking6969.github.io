I get up, I move, I answer when spoken to. Somewhere in the last few months I stopped being the one doing it. I go to work, I say the right things, I nod along, and none of it requires me to actually be there. My hands know the motions. My mouth knows the responses. The part of me that used to have to try has clocked out and nobody noticed the handoff.

That's the strange part — how well it works. Nobody can tell. I show up, I finish the tasks, I laugh at the right moments. It's a good impression of a person. Maybe too good, because it means I don't have to come back and actually be present. It just keeps flying itself.

I used to think autopilot was a relief. Less to carry, less to feel, less deciding. And some days it is — it gets me through things I don't think I could get through awake. But it also means I look up and days are just gone. Weeks. I can't tell you what happened in most of them. Just that I was technically there for it.

I don't know when I'm supposed to take the controls back. I'm not even sure I remember how.

Sometimes I catch a glimpse of myself doing it — mid-sentence, mid-task, watching my own hands from somewhere just behind my eyes — and there's this flicker of *oh, there you are*. Not relief exactly. More like spotting someone you used to know across a crowded room and not being sure they'd want to be caught. The flicker never lasts. Something asks a question, or a task pings, and I'm back in the seat that isn't really a seat, watching the version of me that knows what to say.

People keep telling me I seem fine. That's the part that gets me. Fine is the autopilot's best trick — it doesn't crash, it doesn't even wobble, it just holds altitude forever. Nobody checks on the plane that isn't going down. So I don't get asked how I'm doing in any way that expects a real answer, and I don't offer one, and the loop just continues, smooth and unremarkable, exactly the way it's built to.

I keep thinking there should be a moment — an alarm, a jolt, something that forces my hands back on the controls. But it doesn't work that way. Nobody hands the plane back to you. You just have to notice you're gone and reach for it yourself, and most days I don't have the reach in me. So it flies on. And I let it, because at least it knows where it's going, even if I don't remember agreeing to the destination.
